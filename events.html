9:00 am Hike Into History: Shaker Woodlands Enfield Shaker Museum

Free – $15

Hike Into History

Friday, November 20th at 9:00am

Free With Museum Admission

Meet at the Stone Machine Shop at the Enfield Shaker Museum

Want to explore the Shaker trails while learning about the history of the buildings and structures along the way? Then join us for our monthly Hike into History!
The woods and the outdoors have always been essential to the New England, and especially the New Hampshire identity. The woodlands around the Enfield Shaker Museum have changed a lot since the Shakers arrived in 1793 until they became the wooded trails we know today. Lean how the woodland landscape of New England has changed, what the Shaker lands were used for, and about the trails around the Shaker Museum!
This hike will be outside and in the woods. Please come prepared with water, snacks, appropriate footwear and layers, and be prepared for sun, rain, cold, bugs, and ticks.
Please note this trail will be on NH Fish and Game Trails. Bright colors are encouraged for clothing

PARKING INFORMATION: Parking can be found at the Stone Machine Shop or in the lot for the Enfield Shaker Museum. Please note if you park in the museum lot that you will have to cross Route 4A to reach the trailhead

ACCESSIBILITY: This 2 mile loop style hike will have about 590ft of elevation change. The trail is wide and semi level, but still has some roots, rocks, and washouts divots from washouts, roots, and rocks.

Coming from a distance? Stay at the historic Great Stone Dwelling! Accommodations.

Nov 21 – Nov 22, 2026 Etching with BIG Ground & Acrylic Spray Aquatint at TRPS | 10 AM - 4PM Two Rivers Printmaking Studio - WRJ, VT, White River Jct

Sessions start 10:00 am · $0–$438

Instructor: Briony Morrow-Cribbs

This workshop takes place at Two Rivers Printmaking Studio: 85 N Main St, suite 160, White River Jct, VT, 05001

This two-day workshop explores two essential intaglio techniques — hardground line etching and aquatint — using safer, greener materials. We'll begin by preparing copper plates and coating them with Baldwin's BIG ground, a non-toxic alternative to traditional hardgrounds that produces the same wonderfully crisp etched line. Students will draw into the ground, etch their plates, and pull a first proof on the press.

On day two, we'll return to our plates and apply an acrylic aquatint spray to build tone, texture, and depth. We'll be using acrylic spray in place of traditional rosin aquatint — a safer, healthier approach that also opens up exciting possibilities for creating gradients. Through step etching, students will learn to develop a rich range of values on a single plate, from delicate grays to velvety darks.

By the end of the weekend, participants will leave with at least one proof of their plate and a solid foundation in these versatile processes. This workshop is suitable for beginners, though a foundation in etching will be helpful.

Evergreen Holiday Wreath Workshop

Monday, November 30, 2026 | 4–6 pm

This event is now sold-out; we will try to add an additional workshop date. Please join the waitlist below to be notified if any tickets become available.

You may also be interested in attending our Evergreen Centerpiece Workshop. 

Welcome the holiday season with the fresh scent of evergreens and the joy of creating something beautiful by hand! Join instructors Happy Griffiths and Nan Munsey for a festive, hands-on workshop where you’ll learn to craft your own 14″ evergreen holiday wreath using fresh greens and natural decorations.

This workshop features an innovative fabric-wrapped wreath base that makes wreath-making more accessible and sustainable, giving you a technique you can use to create beautiful wreaths for years to come. Whether you’re a seasoned crafter or trying wreath-making for the first time, you’ll leave with a handmade wreath to adorn your home for the holidays, and the skills to make more in seasons to come!

All materials needed to create your wreath will be provided. Feel free to bring your own scissors and clippers, along with any special decorations you’d like to incorporate.

Enjoy holiday refreshments and a cozy, creative afternoon in the historic Great Stone Dwelling at Enfield Shaker Museum.

Workshop Details

  • Class Size: Limited to 10 participants, advance registration is required
  • Materials: All materials provided to make a 14″ wreath
  • Refreshments: Hot cocoa and cookies served

About the Instructors

Happy Griffiths founded the Enfield Shaker Museum Herb Garden in 1987. An experienced herbalist, Happy brings decades of knowledge about plants, their traditional uses, and their enduring beauty to her work preserving and sharing the garden’s botanical heritage.

Nan Munsey is a longtime Museum garden volunteer and former teacher who brings her creativity and enthusiasm for working with natural materials to this collaborative workshop.

Non Members – $60

The Great Stone Dwelling is wheelchair and walker accessible. Designated accessible parking spaces are available in the parking lot. Both the men’s and women’s restrooms are wheelchair-accessible.

If you require accommodations to participate in this workshop, please contact garden@shakermuseum.org in advance so we can work with you to support your needs.

5 pm - 6 pm "The New Era of Extreme Bionics" with Hugh Herr Filene Auditorium, Moore Building

45min. talk with 15min Q&A followed by a public reception. Free and open to the public

Half the world’s population currently suffers from some form of physical or neurological disability.  But what if there were no such thing as human disability? What if, through the invention and deployment of novel technologies, human disability could be eradicated? Advances in genetics, regenerative & reconstructive medicine, artificial intelligence and robotics are beginning to enable more intimate connections between the human body and machines.  From neural interfaces with the human nervous system, to bionic limbs that move like their biological counterparts, Professor Hugh Herr will discuss Extreme Bionics, a research agenda with global implications for improving the human condition.  Through fundamental advancements in human-machine interaction, society will eliminate disability in this 21st century, and establish the scientific and technological basis for human augmentation, extending human capability beyond innate physiological levels -- cognitively, emotionally and physically.

About Hugh Herr
Hugh Herr is reimagining the relationship between human beings and technology, creating bionic systems that restore mobility, expand human capability, and redefine the boundaries of the body. Recognizing his pioneering contributions to the field, TIME magazine named Dr. Herr the “Leader of the Bionic Age.” His work has helped establish the discipline of biomechatronics, an area of research that integrates human physiology with electromechanical technologies to restore and enhance the human body. A double amputee himself, Herr has led breakthrough advances in bionic limb technology that have transformed the lives of people with physical disabilities around the world. He is Professor of Media Arts and Sciences at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), an Associate Investigator at the McGovern Institute for Brain Research, Director of the MIT Biomechatronics Group, and Co-Director of the K. Lisa Yang Center for Bionics.

Jan 30 – Jan 31, 2027 Introduction to Mokulito Printmaking at TRPS | 10 AM-4 PM Two Rivers Printmaking Studio - WRJ, VT, White River Jct

Sessions start 10:00 am · $300–$500

Instructor: Janey Cathey

This workshop takes place at Two Rivers Printmaking Studio: 85 N Main St, suite 160, White River Jct, VT, 05001

Mokulito,“wood lithography”, is a relatively newly developed process (first done in the 1970’s). Like traditional lithography, the process relies on the principle that oil and water do not mix. Oily substances are used to draw and/or paint an image on the block, the block is soaked with water, and oily printing ink is rolled over it. The printing ink sticks to the oily surfaces only, and then the block is run through the press or hand-printed. Mokulito is much more accessible than traditional limestone lithography because less preparation is required. This type of printmaking is unique and exciting because marks and images may be created on one block of plywood through drawing, painting AND carving, and then all are printed simultaneously. Some printmaking experience is helpful.

2 Sessions | Sat-Sun, Jan 30-31 | 10 AM-4 PM

Janet Cathey received a grant from the Vermont Arts Council in 2024 to study Mokulito printmaking at Art Print Residence in Catalonia, Spain.
